## Night clock run 2026-11-15/16

**Bottom line:** the node clock fix holds (`lost_ms` at most 9 ms, no growth, no jump of minutes), but S3 on the T-Beam
and S4 on the Heltec miss the written criterion `|delta| <= 2 s` at each `{CET}`: the clock gets stepped by 2 to 6 s
per beacon, in both directions. The scatter comes from the `{CET}` beacons that arrive over LoRa, not from the node
clock. CET-AGE-01 passes. Agent-driven, nodes on `afaa2f40` INSTRUMENT, 2 dBm, mesh off, no test traffic sent.

- **Reading:** with WLAN on, DK5EN-1 stays under 0.7 s on the same beacons the T-Beam scatters on; with WLAN off,
  DK5EN-1 scatters like the T-Beam. Hypothesis, not verified: a gateway node also gets `{CET}` from the server stream,
  while a LoRa-only node takes beacons from different origins whose clocks differ by several seconds. The alternating
  sign fits two or more origins better than transit age alone (age would only ever make the clock late).
- **-5716 and -5449 ms:** both are a -5 s whole-second step plus a fraction; `cetStepAllowed` accepts exactly -5 s, so
  this is the designed boundary, not a CET-AGE-01 failure.
- **`[CET];skip;back_s` lines:** not evidenced. `clock_drift.py` records only `[CLOCK]` and `[DISPCLK]` lines. Every
  beacon produced a `[CLOCK];set`, so no skip happened.
- **Tool gaps:** `clock_drift.py` neither records `[CET]` lines (origin and age of the beacon) nor knows an offline
  boot; S4 aborted once with `BootWaitTimeout` (no `[BOOT];ready` without WLAN) and ran through a scratchpad wrapper.
